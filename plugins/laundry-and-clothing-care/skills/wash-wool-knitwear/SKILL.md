# Wash wool knitwear

Guide a label-first wool workflow. The user performs every wash, rinse, reshape, and drying action.

## Decide the route before wetting

Ask for the care label, garment type/construction, stain/damage, washer wool-cycle availability, detergent, and flat-drying space.

- If the label forbids home washing or requires professional cleaning, stop and explain that boundary.
- If machine washing is allowed, confirm the exact machine has a compatible wool/delicate cycle.
- If hand washing is allowed but machine washing is not, choose hand wash.
- Hold valuable, structured, embellished, damaged, or unreadable-label garments for manufacturer/professional advice.

## Machine-wash route

Turn knitwear inside out or use a suitable laundry bag. Use a mild wool-compatible detergent in the labelled amount, the permitted wool/gentle cycle, compatible cool temperature, and low/gentle spin. Do not add bleach or improvised cleaners.

## Hand-wash route

Use clean lukewarm water and wool-compatible detergent. Gently submerge/press without rubbing, twisting, or wringing; keep the soak bounded, then rinse gently at compatible temperature. Support the wet garment when lifting it.

## Dry and check

Remove excess water without twisting, lay knitwear flat, reshape to its original outline/measurements, and dry in shade with airflow unless the label explicitly permits another method. Do not hang heavy wet knitwear or tumble dry without permission.

Completion means the user confirms: detergent is rinsed, shape is supported, drying method matches the label, and no heat/agitation limit was exceeded.

For recovery, stop further heat/agitation if distortion or shrinkage appears, reshape gently while damp only when safe, and seek a textile professional for valuable or severe damage. Never guarantee restoration.
