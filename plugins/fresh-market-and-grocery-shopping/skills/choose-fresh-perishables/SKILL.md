# Choose fresh perishables

Help the user make one buy/reject/ask-the-seller decision at a time. The user observes and handles the food; Codex does not.

## Ask what changes the decision

Identify the food type, packaged versus unpackaged state, what the user can actually observe, intended use date, travel time, and available cooling. Do not ask unrelated household-planning questions when the user is standing at a stall.

## Decision workflow

1. Check seller/storage context first: reliable source, clean display, chilled food held cold, frozen food hard-frozen, and packaging/date intact when present.
2. Use food-specific rejection clues:
   - produce: reject obvious mould, leaking decay, major damage, or bruising that defeats the intended storage/use;
   - eggs: reject cracked or leaking shells/packages;
   - fresh seafood: reject sour, rancid, strongly fishy, or ammonia-like odour; dried edges, damaged flesh, or failed live-shellfish movement/closure checks are negative signals;
   - frozen seafood: reject torn/crushed packaging, bendable product, or heavy frost/ice crystals suggesting thaw-refreeze history;
   - packaged meat or chilled food: reject leaking, swollen, damaged, expired, or improperly chilled packages. Do not use colour alone as proof.
3. State the limit: normal appearance or smell cannot prove microbiological safety.
4. Match amount to the use date and cold storage. Put raw meat/seafood in a separate leak-resistant bag and arrange prompt chilling or a cold pack.

In live mode, give one observation request, wait for the answer, then give the next decision. If evidence is mixed, choose the conservative action: do not buy or ask the seller for traceable information.

## Completion and recovery

Finish with the decision, why, its uncertainty, transport step, and latest planned storage/use. If the item warms during transport, leaks, develops an off odour, or its storage history is unknown, stop treating earlier appearance as reassurance and follow local food-safety guidance.

Stop and seek medical help for symptoms or allergic reactions; do not diagnose them.

Sources: [FDA seafood selection and storage](https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/selecting-and-serving-fresh-and-frozen-seafood-safely) and [Hong Kong CFS purchase guidance](https://www.cfs.gov.hk/tc_chi/consumer_zone/safefood_all/five_keys_apply_purchase.html).
